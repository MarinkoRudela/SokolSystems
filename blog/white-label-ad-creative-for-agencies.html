White-label ad creative for agencies: your options, what they cost, and what to check before you sign

How agencies outsource ad creative under their own name: published prices for each option, and nine things to check in any white-label partner, from file names to AI rules.

By Marinko Rudela · 26 October 2026 · Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

White-label ad creative means another team makes the ads and your agency delivers them to clients as its own. Your clients see your agency, not the partner. For agencies running paid social, it’s usually about capacity: Meta now rewards a steady supply of varied creative (we cover the evidence in our guide to creative fatigue), and most agencies can’t hire designers and video editors as fast as their client list grows.

Here are the main options, what they publish as prices, and what to check before you trust any of them with your clients.

Per piece, Darkroom’s own market estimate is that agencies selling creative by the asset charge $150 to $500 per static ad and $500 to $2,000 per video. That’s a useful yardstick when you work out your margin on resold creative.

Nine things to check in any white-label partner

  1. No trace of them anywhere. Not on the creative, not in file names, not in text files or video credits. Ask to see a real delivery folder.
  2. They never contact your clients. Get it in writing, including what happens if a client finds them.
  3. Who owns the work. You and your client should be free to use everything delivered, for any lawful purpose, with no extra license fees.
  4. Confidentiality. Your clients’ products, results and plans shouldn’t appear in the partner’s portfolio without permission.
  5. Turnaround, defined. “48 hours” can mean a first draft or a finished file, and the clock may start when you send the brief or when they start work. Ask which.
  6. What counts as one request. “Unlimited” plans still define a request. If one ad in five sizes counts as five requests, your capacity is a fifth of what you thought.
  7. Capacity across all your clients. Most plans share one queue across every brand. Work out how many deliveries a month that really means for your client list.
  8. How AI is used, and the rules that come with it. Since October 2024, an FTC rule bans fake testimonials, including AI-generated ones from people who don’t exist, with civil penalties of up to $51,744 per violation. An AI presenter can explain a product; it can’t pose as a customer. Meta also adds an “AI info” label to some ads made or edited with generative AI. Know what your partner makes, and agree with your clients how it will be used.
  9. Faces, voices and materials. Your partner should only use a real person’s likeness with documented consent, and only material your client has the rights to.

How to test a partner

Give a partner one real brief for one client before you commit: ideally their best-performing ad, with “make new versions of this.” Then judge three things: whether it arrived when promised, whether it followed the brief, and whether you’d put your agency’s name on it without editing. Some partners, us included, will make a first set free.

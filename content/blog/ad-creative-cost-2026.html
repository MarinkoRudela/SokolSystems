Freelancers

Upwork’s own cost guides put graphic designers at about $25 an hour on average, with a range of $15 to $150. For video editors, Upwork’s median rates are $10 an hour at entry level, $40 mid-tier and $100 senior.

Makes sense when you need a specific one-off job, like a logo, a single launch video or a set of product photos, and you have time to brief and review it.

Watch for: availability and deadlines vary person to person, most freelancers have one specialty, and you brief each new person from scratch. In 167 reviews and forum posts we read, late or missing work was one of the most common complaints about freelance help.

Design subscriptions

These charge a flat monthly fee for a queue of design requests. ManyPixels lists plans from $699 a month (one daily output, one project at a time) to $2,599 a month for two dedicated part-time designers, with next-day first drafts on its entry plans. Designjoy charges $4,995 a month for one request at a time, with delivery in 48 hours on average, and both let you cancel anytime.

Makes sense when you have a steady flow of general design work: social graphics, decks, landing pages, banners.

Watch for: check what’s included (video, motion and photography vary by plan and provider), whether “turnaround” means a first draft or a finished file, and how many requests run at once.

An in-house team

Hiring gives you the most control, at the highest fixed cost. US median annual pay, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (May 2025), is $62,960 for graphic designers, $75,100 for film and video editors, and $44,660 for photographers. Together that’s $15,227 a month in pay alone, before benefits, payroll taxes, equipment and software.

Makes sense when creative is a full-time, every-day need across many channels and you can keep each person busy.

Creative agencies

Large creative agencies sell budgets or dedicated teams. Superside publishes its prices: from $15,000 a month on its Flex plan, or from $30,000 a month for a dedicated team, plus a $1,000 monthly software fee, on a 12-month term. It says a dedicated team is live in about three weeks.

Makes sense when you’re a larger brand that needs strategy, project management and production at scale, and can commit for a year.

How to choose

  • A one-off job: a freelancer.
  • Steady general design work on a small budget: a design subscription.
  • Ongoing new versions of ads that already work, plus photo and video: a creative subscription built around your winners, like ours.
  • Full-time creative across many channels: an in-house team.
  • Large brand, strategy plus production at scale: an agency.
